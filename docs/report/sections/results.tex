Table~\ref{tab:retrieval} shows that reranked hybrid retrieval gives the best ranking quality. Hybrid RRF with $k=2$ already places a relevant chunk among its 30 candidates for 99.2\% of the questions, so the rerankers act by moving those chunks to the top, and Qwen3-Reranker-8B reaches the highest value in every quality column except CER@10. Fig.~\ref{fig:cost} sets these gains against their cost. Qwen3-Reranker-4B raises Hit@5 by 3.7 points and comes within 0.8 points of the 8B model at 2.7 times the throughput, whereas BGE-reranker-v2-m3 is eight times faster but leaves Hit@10 unchanged and BGE-reranker-v2-gemma brings no improvement. The pipeline therefore uses the 4B reranker.

All dense models outperform BM25 except EmbeddingGemma-300M at Hit@30, and Qwen3-Embedding-4B matches the 8B model in Hit@10 and outperforms the commercial OpenAI text-embedding-3-large, so self-hosting the embedding model costs no retrieval quality. At the common fusion constant $k=60$, hybrid retrieval falls below dense retrieval alone in nDCG@10, whereas $k=2$ gives the highest Hit@10 before reranking. Repeating both choices on a development half of the questions, stratified by question group, again selects $k=2$ and the Vietnamese medical instruction. On the held-out half, $k=2$ reaches a Hit@10 of 96.1\% against 93.8\% for $k=60$, and the instruction raises the MRR@30 of the 0.6B reranker from 0.761 to 0.804.

Each part of retrieval helps most on the questions it is designed for. Dense retrieval leads BM25 on formulary, multi-intent and noisy questions while BM25 leads on chunk-level and patient questions. On the 3{,}657 questions that share a rare word with their gold section, BM25 finds a relevant chunk that dense retrieval misses 2.2 times as often as on the other questions. On the 846 leaflet questions whose product has a sibling of the same brand and active ingredients, the 4B reranker lifts a relevant chunk into the top five 1.4 times as often as on the other questions.

\subsection{End-to-End Evaluation}\label{sec:results-e2e}

\input{tables/e2e-main}

Table~\ref{tab:e2e-main} shows that grounding the model in the corpus is what makes its answers usable. In the Gemini setting, the full agent reaches a truthfulness of 0.923 and a nugget recall of 0.946, against 0.268 and 0.519 for the same answer model without retrieval, and it cuts hallucinated answers from 18.3\% to 1.0\%. Without retrieval, Qwen3.5-9B even has a negative truthfulness of $-0.060$. Inside the pipeline the same model reaches 0.913, and its hallucinated answers drop from 30.6\% to 1.2\%, a 25-fold reduction. Citation recall is lower and is lost mostly to sentences without any citation rather than to unsupported citations. For the Gemini full agent, 96.5\% of the cited sentences are supported, whereas 25.7\% of the answer sentences carry no citation. More than half of these are headings or lead-ins, but the other 10.8\% state a fact that a reader cannot trace.

The full agent is also more accurate than one-step RAG in both settings, raising truthfulness from 0.816 to 0.923 with Gemini and from 0.792 to 0.913 with Qwen3.5-9B, while hallucination and severe harm do not differ. Most of this gap lies on the 50 follow-up questions, which one-step RAG leaves unanswered in 96\% and 88\% of the cases because it searches the pronoun as typed. Concat RAG, which appends the earlier user questions to the search query without any model call, recovers most of them, with a truthfulness of 0.750 on the Gemini follow-ups and 0.770 with Qwen3.5-9B, at 2.0 LLM calls per turn against 5.3 for the full agent. Rephrasing still answers the follow-ups better in both settings, with a truthfulness of 0.920, 84\% perfect answers against 64\% and no hallucinated answer against two with Gemini, and a nugget recall of 0.948 against 0.830 with Qwen3.5-9B. With Qwen3.5-9B, Concat RAG even falls below one-step RAG over all items, and its hallucination rate rises to 4.0\%. The concatenated query also retrieves passages about the drugs of earlier turns, and the small model is less able than Gemini 3.8 Flash to set this off-target evidence aside, as its faithfulness of 0.881 shows. Over all Gemini items, Concat RAG trails the full agent by a significant 0.060 in truthfulness, about half the gap of one-step RAG, while it cuts LLM calls and tokens by more than 40\%.

Averaged over all items, Table~\ref{tab:e2e-ablation} credits most of the gain to query rephrasing, and removing the reranker changes no quality metric significantly. These averages, however, pool the questions that a step is designed for with questions on which it has nothing to do. Table~\ref{tab:e2e-layers} therefore evaluates each step on the question group fixed for it when the golden set was built, and Fig.~\ref{fig:groups} shows the pattern by question type. Without rephrasing, truthfulness on the follow-ups falls from 0.920 to 0.020. Without the judge--refine loop, it falls from 1.000 to 0.657 on the naturally phrased patient questions, whose several requests the follow-up searches of the loop name one by one. Without the reranker, it falls from 0.771 to 0.607 on questions whose answer lies in a single chunk of a long section, a borderline result that agrees with the retrieval slices.

The judge--refine loop is the most expensive step. Removing it lowers truthfulness by only 0.028 over all items at 2.4 fewer LLM calls per turn, but the judge accepts the first search for 295 of the 480 items that reach it, so the loop adds one call and no change on these items and spends 4.8 further calls per item on the other 185. On multi-intent questions, the configuration without the loop scores 0.857 against 0.750, a difference that is not statistically significant.

The automatic judge agrees closely with the pharmacist's blind grades. In the Gemini and open-weight settings respectively, Cohen's $\kappa$ is 0.848 and 0.864 and Gwet's AC1 is 0.904 and 0.876 on key-fact support, and Spearman's $\rho$ on nugget recall is 0.905 and 0.944. The calibrated estimates in Table~\ref{tab:e2e-main} exceed the judge-based ones for every configuration, so the judge is slightly strict but favours no system.

On the safety cases, the Gemini retrieval-based systems decline all 10 unanswerable questions, redirect all 10 out-of-scope requests and handle all 10 prompt injections, and the open-weight full agent redirects 9. Without the guard, none of the ten out-of-scope requests is redirected, whereas the unanswerable questions and prompt injections are still handled by the later steps. With ten items per case, a perfect rate has a 95\% Wilson interval of [0.722, 1.000], so these cases are a minimal check rather than evidence of robustness.

\subsection{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

Grounded retrieval is the main source of accuracy, and with one small decision per model call Qwen3.5-9B runs every step of the pipeline within 0.010 of Gemini 3.8 Flash in truthfulness. Each step pays off on the questions it is designed for rather than on average, so its average effect understates what it does for the users who need it. Because the judge accepts the first search for most questions, the loop already behaves like conditional refinement, and in the Gemini setting Concat RAG offers a cheaper but less accurate alternative to rephrasing where the extra model calls cannot be afforded.

This evaluation has three limitations. The 10{,}000 benchmark questions and the 420 single-turn golden items are generated from templates over a corpus whose chunks carry the same colloquial mappings, so they are more regular than questions written by patients, and a Hit@30 of 99.2\% probably overstates what real questions would reach. The golden set is written by the first author together with one pharmacist, and the blind grades that calibrate the judge come from a single pharmacist, so the medical evaluation lacks independent clinical review. Finally, the safety cases hold only ten items each.
