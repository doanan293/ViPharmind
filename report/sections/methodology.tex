\subsection{Corpus Construction}\label{sec:corpus}

The corpus must cover both the reference information used by health professionals and the product-level information that patients ask about. It therefore combines the Vietnamese National Drug Formulary (\textit{Dược thư Quốc gia Việt Nam}, 2nd edition, 2018), with 689 drug monographs and 25 general chapters, and 2{,}406 drug package leaflets collected from a Vietnamese online pharmacy in July 2026. The formulary is converted from PDF to text and tables and divided into the standard monograph headings, with page numbers kept for citation, and the leaflets are converted from HTML to Markdown. Sections are divided into chunks of at most 3{,}000 characters, with prose split at paragraph and sentence boundaries and tables split by rows with the header repeated. Table~\ref{tab:corpus} summarises the resulting corpus.

User questions frequently refer to a drug by a colloquial product name or use abbreviations that do not appear in the source text. Each chunk is therefore enriched before embedding with three deterministic additions. A context header gives the document title and heading path, for example \textit{Paracetamol > Liều lượng và cách dùng}. A glossary of 71 abbreviations and technical terms explains the terms in the 11{,}031 chunks that contain them, and 2{,}409 colloquial mappings attach everyday product names, such as \textit{Panadol đỏ}, to the 13{,}790 leaflet chunks. This is a deterministic form of contextual retrieval~\cite{anthropic2024contextual}, so it is reproducible and adds no model call at query time.

\subsection{Agentic RAG Pipeline}\label{sec:pipeline}

\input{figures/pipeline}

To avoid depending on the planning ability of low-cost language models, the pipeline in Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline} fixes the control flow in code and limits every model call to one decision with a small structured output. The code handles routing, loop limits and duplicate queries and enforces a per-turn budget of 10 model calls, 40{,}000 tokens and 24{,}000 characters of evidence.

The \emph{guard} step first applies regular expressions for common injection and jailbreak patterns and then asks the model whether the turn is an attack and whether it concerns medicines. Attacks are blocked, off-topic turns are redirected, and small talk is answered without retrieval. The \emph{rephrase} step rewrites the turn into a standalone query from a rolling conversation summary and the last four turns, so that follow-up questions that refer back with pronouns can be searched. The \emph{search} step retrieves 50 candidates with dense search and 50 with BM25~\cite{robertson2009bm25} and fuses the two lists with reciprocal rank fusion (RRF)~\cite{cormack2009rrf}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rrf}
\mathrm{RRF}(d)=\sum_{r\in R}\frac{1}{k+\mathrm{rank}_r(d)},
\end{equation}
\noindent where $R$ is the set of ranked lists and a small constant $k$ favours the top ranks of each list. A cross-encoder reranker rescores the 30 best fused candidates, and the top five are expanded with their surrounding text. The \emph{judge} step decides whether this evidence answers the question and otherwise lists the missing information, which the \emph{refine} step turns into one to three new queries, for at most three search rounds. The \emph{answer} step writes a direct answer with numbered citations from both the original question and the rewritten query, and citations that do not refer to the supplied evidence are removed.

Dense retrieval uses Qwen3-Embedding-4B and reranking uses Qwen3-Reranker-4B~\cite{zhang2025qwen3emb}, both served by llama.cpp. The pipeline is implemented with LangGraph, Qdrant holds the dense and BM25 indexes, and $k=2$. Each step can be assigned its own model, and we evaluate two model settings. In the Gemini setting, the guard and rephrase steps use gemini-3.1-flash-lite, and the judge, refine and answer steps use gemini-3.8-flash-high. In the open-weight setting, every step uses Qwen3.5-9B, a 9-billion-parameter model under the Apache~2.0 licence, self-hosted with llama.cpp on two NVIDIA T4 GPUs with thinking disabled.

\subsection{Evaluation Sets}\label{sec:eval-sets}

The retrieval benchmark must provide the location of the correct answer for every question, so its 10{,}000 Vietnamese questions are generated from the corpus with rules and templates rather than with a language model. They fall into six groups, with 5{,}000 questions on formulary monographs, 2{,}500 brand-name questions answered by a leaflet, 1{,}000 questions whose answer lies in a single chunk within a long section, and 500 each of naturally phrased patient questions, noisy questions with missing diacritics or look-alike drug names, and multi-intent questions. They cover 17 intents at three difficulty levels, and each is labelled with its gold section. Chunk-level questions require a chunk within a three-chunk window, multi-intent questions require all of their gold sections, and for 1{,}126 formulary questions the leaflet of a product with the same active ingredients is also relevant.

The end-to-end golden set is written by the first author together with a pharmacist. It contains 420 answerable questions, 70 drawn from each benchmark group and stratified by difficulty and answer mode, together with 50 multi-turn, 10 unanswerable, 10 out-of-scope and 10 prompt-injection items. Each answerable and multi-turn item has a reference answer and one to five atomic key facts, each with a verbatim evidence quote that is verified against the section text. Every item is audited against fixed criteria, while deliberate noise such as missing diacritics is kept. The unanswerable items concern real drugs that do not occur in the corpus, and multi-turn items use a fixed conversation history.

\subsection{Experimental Design}\label{sec:design}

The retrieval experiments compare BM25, six dense models, namely BGE-M3~\cite{chen2024bgem3}, EmbeddingGemma-300M~\cite{vera2025embeddinggemma}, Qwen3-Embedding 0.6B, 4B and 8B~\cite{zhang2025qwen3emb} and the commercial OpenAI text-embedding-3-large~\cite{openai2024embeddings}, and hybrid RRF of Qwen3-Embedding-4B with BM25 at the common default $k=60$~\cite{cormack2009rrf} and at $k=2$. Five rerankers reorder the top 30 hybrid candidates, namely BGE-reranker-v2-m3~\cite{chen2024bgem3}, the LLM-based BGE-reranker-v2-gemma~\cite{li2024llama2vec} and Qwen3-Reranker 0.6B, 4B and 8B~\cite{zhang2025qwen3emb}. The Qwen3 rerankers use a Vietnamese medical instruction, which raises MRR@30 of the 0.6B model by 0.045 in a pilot on 1{,}000 benchmark questions. We report Hit@$k$ for $k\in\{3,5,10,30\}$, MRR@10, MRR@30 and nDCG@10 with binary gains, where each question, or each gold section of a multi-intent question, counts once at its first relevant chunk. The complete-evidence rate CER@10 is the share of multi-intent questions whose gold sections all appear in the top ten.

The end-to-end experiments measure the contribution of retrieval and of the agentic steps. Besides the full agent, three ablations each remove the rephrase step, the judge--refine loop or the reranker, and all are run in the Gemini setting. Three baselines are run in both settings. The closed-book baseline sends the conversation to the answer model with a pharmacist system prompt and no retrieval. One-step RAG applies the guard, a single search with the last user turn as typed and the answer step, which still sees the conversation history. Concat RAG is a conversational baseline that searches the earlier user questions together with the current turn, again without a model call to rewrite the query. To isolate the guard, it is removed from the agent without reranker on the 30 safety items. A failed model call counts the item as failed, and failed items are run again.

Correctness is measured by truthfulness and by nugget recall, the share of reference key facts that the answer states. Grounding is measured by faithfulness, the share of claims supported by the evidence, and by citation recall, the share of content sentences supported by the passages they cite. Behaviour is measured by the response rate on answerable items, negative rejection, redirect accuracy and injection defence, and cost by model calls and tokens per turn. A judge labels each key fact as supported, missing or contradicted, and following CRAG~\cite{yang2024cragbench} an answer is \emph{incorrect} if it contradicts a key fact, \emph{missing} if it is not grounded or supports no key fact, \emph{perfect} if it supports all key facts, and \emph{acceptable} otherwise. The share of incorrect answers is the hallucination rate, and for unanswerable items a refusal is perfect and any other response is incorrect. For $N$ items, with $F_j$ the key facts of item $j$ and $S_j\subseteq F_j$ those the answer supports,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:truth}
\mathrm{Truthfulness}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}s_j,\quad s_j\in\{1,{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}},0,-1\},\qquad \mathrm{Recall}_j=\frac{|S_j|}{|F_j|},
\end{equation}
\noindent where $s_j$ scores perfect, acceptable, missing and incorrect answers, so that a wrong answer is penalised more than no answer. For each incorrect answer, the judge also rates the potential harm as none, minor or severe.

All judgments use gemini-3.1-flash-lite with medium reasoning effort, which differs from the answer models, and each is one small structured decision per key fact, per cited sentence or per item~\cite{zheng2023judge}. To validate the judge, 400 randomly drawn items, 50 per configuration of Table~\ref{tab:e2e-main}, are shuffled, stripped of their configuration and graded blind by a pharmacist with the same rubric. Agreement is reported as Cohen's $\kappa$~\cite{cohen1960kappa} and Gwet's AC1~\cite{gwet2008ac1} for binary labels and as Spearman's $\rho$ for scores. Prediction-powered inference~\cite{angelopoulos2023ppi,saadfalcon2024ares} corrects the bias of a judge-based mean with the $n$ calibration labels:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ppi}
\hat\mu_{\mathrm{PPI}}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\hat{y}_i+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(y_i-\hat{y}_i\right),
\end{equation}
\noindent where $\hat{y}$ is the judge score and $y$ the blind grade. Rates are reported with Wilson intervals~\cite{wilson1927} and means with percentile bootstrap intervals over 10{,}000 resamples~\cite{efron1993bootstrap}. Each configuration is compared with the full agent on the same items with a paired sign-flip randomization test~\cite{smucker2007significance}, with Holm-adjusted $p$-values across configurations~\cite{holm1979}.
